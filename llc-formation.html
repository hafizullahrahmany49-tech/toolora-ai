US business setup guide

How to Start an LLC in the USA

A practical overview of the usual steps, costs to investigate, and questions founders—including people living outside the United States—should answer before forming a company.

Important: This is general educational information, not legal, tax, immigration, or accounting advice. Requirements, fees, and deadlines vary by state and situation. Verify current rules with official agencies and a qualified professional when needed.

1. Decide whether an LLC fits your needs

A limited liability company (LLC) is a business entity created under state law. It can offer liability separation and flexible management, but it does not remove every personal risk or guarantee a particular tax outcome. Consider your customers, business activities, ownership, home-country rules, expected costs, and whether another structure would be more suitable.

2. Choose a state based on real needs

Do not choose a state based only on a popular online claim about privacy or low taxes. Compare the state where you actually operate with any alternative state, including initial filing fees, annual reports, franchise or other taxes, registered-agent costs, and whether registering in another state could create additional obligations.

  • Where will the business be managed and operate?
  • Does the state require annual reports or recurring fees?
  • Will you need to register as a foreign entity in another state?
  • What are the public-record and privacy rules?

3. Check the business name

Search the chosen state's official business registry and review naming rules before filing. Also check trademarks, domain availability, and whether the name could confuse customers with an existing business. A state name approval is not the same as trademark clearance.

4. Appoint a registered agent

Most states require an LLC to name a registered agent with a physical address in that state to receive official notices and legal documents. You may qualify to serve yourself if you meet the state's rules, or you may hire a service. Compare renewal prices, privacy, mail handling, coverage, cancellation terms, and the exact services included.

5. File formation documents

Submit the required formation document—often called Articles of Organization—to the appropriate state agency and pay the state filing fee. Keep the confirmation and approved documents. Some businesses also prepare an operating agreement, even where it is not required to be filed with the state.

6. Research EIN, banking, and taxes

An EIN is a federal tax ID issued by the IRS. The correct application route depends on the applicant's circumstances; an EIN is not the same as an LLC registration. Banks and payment services apply their own eligibility, identity-verification, and country restrictions. An LLC does not automatically guarantee a bank account, payment processing, visa, or tax exemption.

7. Budget for ongoing compliance

Before registering, estimate the full first-year and recurring costs: state filing, registered-agent renewal, annual or biennial reports, possible state taxes, accounting, licenses, and federal or state tax filings. Owners living outside the US may also have reporting obligations in their country of residence. Get advice tailored to your facts.

Should you use a formation service?

A commercial provider may help prepare documents or manage administrative steps, but compare what is actually included. Separate government fees from service fees, check renewals and upsells, read refund terms, and verify the provider's reputation. You can often file directly with the relevant state agency.
