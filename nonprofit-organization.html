How to Research Starting a Nonprofit in the US

A high-level roadmap for understanding nonprofit formation and federal tax-exempt status.

Key distinction: Creating a nonprofit corporation under state law and receiving federal tax-exempt recognition are separate steps. Incorporation alone does not automatically make donations tax-deductible or grant exemption from federal taxes.

1. Define the mission and activities

Write a clear charitable, educational, religious, scientific, or other eligible purpose, and identify the people or communities the organization will serve. The organization must operate in a way that meets the legal requirements for the status it seeks.

2. Check state formation rules

Review the state’s nonprofit corporation requirements, name availability, articles of incorporation, registered-agent rules, board requirements, and recurring reports. Some organizations may need state charitable-solicitation registration before fundraising; requirements depend on the states involved.

3. Plan governance

  • Identify suitable directors and officers.
  • Prepare bylaws and conflict-of-interest procedures where appropriate.
  • Keep accurate minutes, financial records, and decisions.
  • Set up transparent controls for donations, spending, and related-party transactions.

4. Apply for an EIN if needed

An Employer Identification Number is a federal tax identifier. Follow the current IRS process and do not confuse obtaining an EIN with receiving tax-exempt status.

5. Research federal tax-exempt recognition

Some organizations apply to the IRS using Form 1023 or, if eligible, Form 1023-EZ. Eligibility, required documents, fees, and deadlines must be checked against current IRS instructions. Not every organization qualifies, and filing an application does not guarantee approval.

6. Budget for ongoing compliance

Consider annual federal information returns or notices, state reports, fundraising rules, accounting, payroll if staff are hired, and any licenses relevant to the activities. Missing required filings can put status at risk.

Official resources

Begin with the IRS Charities and Nonprofits hub and the SBA business-structure guide. For state requirements, use the relevant state government’s official website.

Educational information only, not legal or tax advice. Nonprofit rules are specific to the organization, state, and intended tax status; consult qualified professionals as needed.
