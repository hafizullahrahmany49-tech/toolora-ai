How to Choose a State for Your LLC

A practical starting guide for founders, including people who live outside the United States.

Short answer: Start by checking the state where you actually operate, have staff, or conduct business. Forming in a famous business-friendly state is not automatically cheaper or better; registering in another state may mean extra filings and fees.

1. Start with where your business operates

If you live or operate in a particular state, investigate that state's LLC rules first. If you form in one state but conduct business in another, you may need foreign qualification there, which can create additional fees, reports, and registered-agent requirements. The exact test depends on state law.

2. Compare the total cost—not just the formation fee

  • Initial filing fee.
  • Annual or biennial reports and recurring state fees.
  • Franchise or other state taxes, where applicable.
  • Registered-agent service costs and renewal prices.
  • Extra registration costs if you operate in multiple states.

3. Consider taxes and administration

An LLC is a state-law entity; federal tax treatment is a separate question. Tax outcomes depend on ownership, activity, elections, and the laws that apply. Do not choose a state based only on a headline claiming “no tax.”

4. If you live outside the US

Check whether the state filing process supports your situation, what address and registered-agent details are required, how you will receive official notices, and whether you can meet banking, tax, and ongoing reporting obligations. State formation does not guarantee a bank account, visa, or tax exemption.

5. A simple comparison checklist

  • Where will the business actually operate?
  • What is the full first-year and recurring cost?
  • Are annual reports or franchise taxes required?
  • Will you need to register in another state too?
  • Can you maintain a reliable registered-agent address?
  • Have you checked official state guidance and relevant tax advice?

Official resources

Start with the US Small Business Administration's business-structure guide and the relevant Secretary of State website for each state being considered.
