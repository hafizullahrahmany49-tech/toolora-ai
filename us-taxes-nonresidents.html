US Tax Basics for Nonresident Business Owners

A starting checklist for people outside the United States who own or plan to form a US business.

There is no one-size-fits-all answer. Tax duties depend on entity classification, ownership, income type, business activity, US trade or business connections, treaty rules, and the owner’s tax residence. A US LLC does not automatically mean “no US taxes” or “no filings.”

Topics to clarify early

  • Entity classification: A single-member LLC and a multi-member LLC can have different default federal tax classifications; elections can change treatment.
  • Income and activity: US-source income, effectively connected income, and other income categories can be treated differently.
  • Information returns: Some foreign-owned US entities may have special information-reporting obligations even when they owe little or no income tax. Penalties for missed filings can be significant.
  • Individual obligations: Owners may have separate filing duties depending on their status and income.
  • State and local rules: State income/franchise taxes, annual reports, sales tax, payroll, and local requirements may also apply.
  • Home-country obligations: Your country of residence may tax foreign income or require reporting of foreign ownership and accounts.

Prepare these facts before seeking advice

  • Where do you live and where is the business managed?
  • What entity type and tax classification do you have?
  • Who owns the business, and where do the owners reside?
  • What does the business sell, and where are customers and work located?
  • What tax identification numbers and filings already exist?
  • What deadlines apply for the current tax year?

Use official sources

Review the IRS International Taxpayers resource, the IRS business-structures guide, and any relevant instructions for the specific forms that apply to your entity.

Because international tax rules can be technical and deadlines matter, consider a qualified US tax professional with experience serving nonresident owners. Confirm whether they also understand your country’s rules.

Educational information only, not tax or legal advice. This page is not a personalized determination of your filing obligations.
