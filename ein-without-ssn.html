EIN application guide

How to Research an EIN Application Without an SSN

Learn what an Employer Identification Number is, what non-US business owners should check, and where to confirm the current IRS process before applying.

Important: This page is educational, not tax or legal advice. IRS procedures and eligibility can change. Follow current official instructions for your specific situation.

What is an EIN?

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). Eligible businesses and other entities may need one for federal tax administration, hiring employees, and certain banking or business activities. An EIN is not an SSN, does not grant immigration status, and does not automatically make a business eligible for a bank account.

Can you apply without an SSN?

Some applicants who are not eligible for a Social Security Number may still be able to request an EIN, but the correct method depends on the applicant and responsible party. The online EIN application has eligibility restrictions; do not assume every international applicant can use it. Read the IRS instructions for applicants without an SSN or ITIN and follow the current procedure they specify.

Prepare before applying

  • Confirm the legal name and entity type of the business.
  • Identify the responsible party and gather the details requested by current IRS instructions.
  • Check whether the entity has already been assigned an EIN to avoid unnecessary duplicate applications.
  • Review the official IRS form and submission instructions that apply to your situation.
  • Keep copies of submitted documents and any IRS confirmation.

Is the EIN application free?

The IRS does not charge a fee to issue an EIN. Be careful with websites that make it appear that a government fee is required. Private companies may charge for assistance or bundled formation services, but using a paid intermediary is not the same as paying the IRS for an EIN. Check exactly what a service includes before paying.

What if you need help?

If the instructions are unclear or your ownership structure is complex, contact the IRS through its official channels or consult a qualified tax professional familiar with international business owners. Do not rely on social media promises of guaranteed approval or guaranteed tax results.

After receiving an EIN

Keep the EIN notice securely with your business records. An EIN alone does not complete state formation, annual reports, tax returns, licenses, or bank onboarding. Check which ongoing federal, state, and home-country obligations apply to your business.
