LLC vs Corporation: What’s the Difference?

A beginner-friendly comparison of two common US business structures.

There is no universal winner. The right structure depends on ownership plans, management preferences, fundraising goals, state law, and tax circumstances. This guide is a starting point, not an individualized recommendation.
TopicLLCCorporation
OwnershipUsually described in terms of members and membership interests.Usually described in terms of shareholders and shares.
ManagementCan be member-managed or manager-managed, depending on the governing documents and state law.Typically uses a board of directors and officers under corporate law.
FormalitiesOften has fewer required internal formalities, but records and state filings still matter.Often requires more structured governance, records, and corporate formalities.
FundraisingCan raise capital, but ownership arrangements may be less familiar to some institutional investors.Share-based ownership can fit many investor and equity-compensation structures.
Federal tax treatmentMay be treated as disregarded entity, partnership, or corporation depending on ownership and elections.May be taxed as a C corporation, or qualify for an S corporation election if eligibility rules are met.

Questions to ask before choosing

  • Will there be one owner or several?
  • Do you expect outside investors or employee equity?
  • What recordkeeping and governance can you maintain?
  • How will federal, state, and possibly international tax rules apply?
  • What are the formation and recurring costs in the state you are considering?

Important tax distinction

“LLC” describes a legal structure under state law, not one single federal tax status. An LLC may elect corporate tax treatment in some circumstances. S corporation status has eligibility restrictions and is not available to every owner or entity. Confirm the current rules with the IRS and a qualified tax professional before relying on a tax comparison.
