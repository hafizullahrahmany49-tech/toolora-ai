How to Research a US Business Bank Account

A practical preparation checklist for new businesses and founders outside the United States.

Important: Forming a US LLC or obtaining an EIN does not guarantee that a bank or fintech provider will open an account. Eligibility, identity checks, address rules, supported countries, and risk reviews vary by provider.

Documents providers may request

  • Government-issued identity documents for owners and controlling persons.
  • Formation documents and evidence that the entity is active.
  • EIN confirmation or other tax identification details appropriate to the applicant.
  • Business address, contact details, ownership information, and nature of business.
  • Information about expected transactions, source of funds, customers, and operating countries.

These are examples, not a universal checklist. A provider may request different or additional documents.

Questions to ask before applying

  • Does the provider explicitly accept residents of your country and your entity type?
  • Can all owners complete identity verification remotely, or is an in-person visit required?
  • Which countries, currencies, and payment rails are supported?
  • Are there monthly fees, minimum balances, incoming-wire fees, or foreign-exchange costs?
  • Can the account be used for your actual business model and expected transactions?
  • What happens if the application is declined, and are any deposits refundable?

Protect yourself

Use official provider websites, read the eligibility terms before paying an intermediary, and never submit false addresses or ownership details. Do not pay anyone who promises guaranteed approval. A personal payment wallet is not necessarily a substitute for a business bank account or suitable merchant-processing setup.

Next steps

First complete the legal and tax research relevant to your business. Then shortlist providers whose published eligibility rules match your country, company, and ownership. Contact the provider directly if any requirement is unclear.
