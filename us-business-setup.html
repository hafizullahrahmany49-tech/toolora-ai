LLC and EIN: What Non-US Founders Should Know

A beginner-friendly overview of common US business terms and the questions to research before choosing a formation service or submitting an application.

Important: This page is general education, not legal, tax, immigration, or accounting advice. US rules and state requirements change; confirm your situation with official sources or a qualified professional.

What is an LLC?

A limited liability company (LLC) is a type of business entity formed under state law. Its rules, fees, reporting obligations, and tax treatment can differ by state and by the owners' circumstances. Forming an LLC is not a guarantee of profit, a bank account, a visa, or permission to work in the United States.

What is an EIN?

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is a federal tax identification number issued by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for eligible business and tax purposes. Whether and how a person applies depends on the circumstances. An EIN is not the same as an SSN and does not itself establish immigration status or work authorization.

Can someone outside the US form an LLC?

Non-US residents may be able to form certain US business entities, but the right choice depends on the business, state, owners, tax obligations, and practical needs. Before proceeding, consider:

  • Which state’s rules and ongoing fees apply?
  • Is a registered agent required, and what does the service cost?
  • What federal, state, and possibly foreign tax filings may apply?
  • Can your intended bank and payment providers serve your country and business type?
  • What documents and identity checks may be required?
  • Will you need professional legal or tax advice?

How to research EIN requirements

Start with the IRS’s official instructions and application information. Non-US applicants may have a different process from applicants with a US tax identification number, and the correct route depends on eligibility and current IRS guidance.

Compare formation services carefully

If you consider a commercial service, compare the actual state filing fee, service fee, registered-agent renewal, annual report support, refund policy, and what is or is not included. Avoid providers that promise guaranteed banking, guaranteed tax outcomes, or guaranteed income.

What about a UK Ltd?

A UK private limited company (Ltd) follows a separate legal and reporting framework. It should not be treated as interchangeable with a US LLC. Anyone considering a UK company should review current Companies House guidance and relevant tax obligations before making a decision.

Useful next steps

Write down your business purpose, where you live, where your customers are, how you expect to get paid, and what ongoing costs you can afford. Then verify the relevant rules before paying a formation service.
